Getting started

1. System purpose and scope

Purpose. FUTURE is a curriculum–labour-market alignment platform. It prepares and explains governed evidence so that curriculum administrators, analysts, academics and governance reviewers can make better-informed programme decisions. It is decision support — it does not replace curriculum committees, and nothing is applied to a curriculum automatically.

In scope. Curriculum evidence intake and validation, labour-market source ingestion, skill extraction and mapping review, demand-signal generation and the Demand Outlook, recommendation building with human-gated approval, decision history, and exportable reports and saved snapshots. Every record is checksummed, versioned and traceable to its source and ingestion job.

Out of scope (and not claimed). Automatic curriculum change, empirical validity of synthetic or trial-UAT model outputs, institution-specific CHE/DHET evidence (unverified until obtained), and independent expert validation (deferred).

The four evidence classes in the banner above keep these distinctions visible throughout every section of this reference.

How the FUTURE Platform works

Input – Processing – Output at a glance

Input

Evidence comes in

  • Curriculum: prospectuses, study guides, syllabi and module descriptors via PDF upload, API import or CPUT prospectus import.
  • Labour-market trends: StatsSA QLFS and CHE VitalStats publications.
  • Current jobs: uploaded job-advert datasets and simulated job-board feeds (synthetic board and the Adzuna API source under academic research permission).
→

Processing

The system makes sense of it

  • Every record is checksummed, versioned and traceable to its source and ingestion job.
  • Text is cleaned, normalised and linked to canonical skills with confidence scores.
  • Only approved mappings promote to demand evidence; datasets are fingerprint-locked before any training.
  • The baseline alignment model and demand signals prepare interpretable, evidence-backed outputs.
→

Output

People decide with evidence

  • Alignment scores and skill-gap summaries per programme and module.
  • Demand outlooks with confidence levels and stated limitations.
  • Evidence-backed recommendations that require human approval before action.
  • Auditable decision history and exportable reports for committees.

The platform is decision support: it prepares and explains evidence, but qualified staff remain responsible for interpretation, approval and action. Nothing is applied to a curriculum automatically.

Model Lab evidence and lifecycle

Candidate registry, gates, and promotion

Successful training creates an immutable candidate registry entry; it does not automatically make the model active. Candidate metrics, declared non-leaking baselines, chronological/grouped folds, threshold/calibration evidence, and TreeSHAP output remain visible when promotion gates are blocked. Select a registry candidate, run Check Gates, attach genuine automated-test evidence, evaluate it, obtain authorised approval, and promote only if every gate passes. Durable Model Jobs and Training Job Status show the same server-side jobs at detailed and summary levels.

Current technical result (verified 1 October 2026): the latest reviewed snapshot has 993 rows and 40 positive examples (4.03%). Worst chronological-fold F1 is 0, mean chronological-fold F1 is 0.0192 and ECE is 0.1184. The candidate is retained as technical evidence and is not promoted. Current audited recommendations disclose canonical_signal_weighted_baseline_v2 as their method; the portal must not imply that they were produced by the blocked XGBoost candidate.

7. End-to-end operating procedure

The safe operating sequence moves evidence from source intake to a governed decision. Each step records who acted, what was decided, and which evidence supported it, so the trail is auditable end to end.

StepAction and portal location
1Upload and validate curriculum. Import official curriculum documents (prospectuses, study guides, syllabi, module descriptors) under Curriculum Documents; confirm each derived Subject Profile Validation record in Data Operations.
2Import and normalise labour-market evidence. StatsSA QLFS and CHE VitalStats publications, uploaded job-advert datasets, the synthetic job-board feed, and the Adzuna API source under Data Operations.
3Generate and review mappings. Link curriculum and labour-market text to canonical skills in Skill-Gap; approve or reject candidates in the mapping review workbench. Only approved mappings promote to demand evidence.
4Validate curriculum evidence. Inspect extracted chunks in the validation window; approve, request Changes required, or Reject with a short evidence-based note (part of the audit trail).
5Generate labelling tasks. Create independent alignment-label tasks with reviewer pairs and adjudication for disagreements.
6Lock the reviewed-label dataset. Fingerprint-lock the snapshot (labour-ds-v1-<digest16>) for training.
7Train and evaluate. Train candidates, run Check Gates, attach genuine automated-test evidence and retain the complete result. Promote in Model Lab only when every gate passes; a failed candidate remains valid technical evidence.
8Recommend and decide. Review the evidence dossier, record approve / reject / modify with rationale; Curriculum Approver grants final academic approval; nothing is applied automatically.
9Report and export. In Recommendations, confirm all four committee-readiness gates and select Generate committee evidence pack. Preserve the report ID, payload SHA-256 and PDF; use Reports & Exports for other saved snapshots and formats.

Curriculum workflow

Prepare one subject end to end: upload an authorised subject guide, syllabus, module descriptor, curriculum map, prospectus or qualification standard under Curriculum Documents; wait for extraction and confirm the document is active in Curriculum Evidence. Validate the structured Subject Profile (institution, faculty, department, programme/qualification, subject code and name, evidence year, NQF level, credits, purpose, prerequisites). Validate the document evidence against the visible extracted chunks — approve, or choose Changes required / Reject with a short note that becomes audit history. Resolve duplicate or alias subject codes before proceeding, then Generate Evidence Mappings; high-confidence matches auto-approve, uncertain candidates enter the human review queue.

Curriculum placement in this page: requirements in Section 3, roles in Section 2, quality controls in Section 9, provenance in Section 6. Step-by-step tasks: Help Manual → "Complete the curriculum evidence workflow", "Validate an uploaded curriculum subject profile", "Verify the curriculum governance chain".

Labour-market workflow

Run the demand side: import vacancies under Data Operations → Labour (Adzuna API source within enforced bounds — page size 1–50, pages 1–250, at most 250 pages per run, rate-limited; or the synthetic provider with no credentials), under written academic research permission with attribution required. Use Preview request count before any run; saved postings list 50 at a time. Run Process skills, review candidate mappings against the actual advert text, then Generate demand — enabled only once approved mappings exist, and only approved mappings promote into demand signals and evidence. Import StatsSA QLFS quarters (public source) for time-indexed context and view the result in Demand Outlook.

Labour placement in this page: providers and bounds in Section 8, model training in Section 10, recommendations in Section 11. Step-by-step tasks: Help Manual → "Run the labour-market workflow", "Import and validate Stats SA QLFS evidence", "Test the Adzuna connector during the provider trial", "View the labour-market provider readiness report" (“Provider readiness” in Section 8).

9. Quality controls
  • Checksums and versioning. Every record stores a checksum and version and is traceable to its source and ingestion job, so a wrong or corrupted source cannot silently replace evidence.
  • Subject Profile Validation. The structured profile used for reports is explicitly confirmed before release; the original upload and extracted chunks stay preserved for audit.
  • Curriculum Evidence Validation. Reviewers approve, request changes, or reject a document version against visible extracted chunks; each decision carries a short note and becomes audit history.
  • Independent labelling. Two reviewers label independently and disagreements go to adjudication; generated model scores are never shown to reviewers and never used as labels.
  • Mapping review workbench. Only approved candidate mappings promote to demand evidence; rejected mappings are excluded, not hidden.
  • Model promotion gates. Candidates carry metrics, declared baselines, chronological splits, threshold/calibration evidence, and TreeSHAP output; Check Gates requires genuine automated-test evidence before promotion.
  • Evidence classes. The page banner distinguishes technical UAT, synthetic, public evidence and deferred validation so that claims never exceed what was actually recorded.

10. Model training and evaluation

Reviewed labels only. Supervised alignment training uses a SHA-256-locked reviewed-label dataset, never ad-hoc labels and never model-generated scores. A labour-market mapping is not an alignment-training row: each training row represents one reviewed curriculum-demand comparison with its label, provenance and grouping/time fields.

Evaluation. Candidates are assessed with chronological cross-validation or walk-forward folds, threshold/calibration evidence, and TreeSHAP explanations. The LSTM forecasting model is always shown against transparent baseline demand signals and its data-depth maturity status.

Candidate identity and fingerprint. Every candidate is recorded with its exact dataset fingerprint and reviewed-label snapshot version. The Model Lab joins the metrics record to the model registry at read time (non-destructively) so the successful candidate, its dataset fingerprint, its lifecycle state and the experimental-UAT disclosure are unambiguous, while historical failed jobs remain visible and every prior candidate is retained append-only.

Current dataset and evaluation. The latest locked reviewed snapshot contains 993 rows with 40 positive examples (4.03%). Chronological/grouped validation reports worst-fold F1 = 0, mean chronological-fold F1 = 0.0192 and ECE = 0.1184. Some boundaries cannot preserve both group isolation and class coverage because positives are sparse. These are honest technical findings, not a software failure.

Promotion. A candidate is promoted only when gates pass: stable chronological/grouped folds, acceptable comparison with a non-leaking baseline, calibration within the declared threshold, genuine automated-test evidence, completed evaluation, authorised approval, and Administrator promotion. The current XGBoost candidate remains unpromoted. More genuinely distinct curriculum programmes/institutions and positive examples are future validation work; duplicated vacancies or rows must not be used to inflate performance.

synthetic A synthetic development snapshot proves the workflow executes end to end (for example run_model_training_readiness_uat.py --seed 42 --size 600), but its metrics must not be reported as empirical research findings.

11. Recommendation and decision support

Recommendations are built from the evidence dossier: approved mapping evidence, forecast context, alignment explanation, and the decision history for the subject. Users record a decision (approve / reject / modify) with rationale.

  • Final academic approval (recommendation.academic_approve) is held only by Curriculum Approvers; Administrators are deliberately excluded and cannot exercise or bypass it.
  • Zero-evidence gate. A recommendation with no resolvable labour-market evidence is marked non-actionable with a reason. It can be rejected but never approved until evidence generation re-supports it; existing zero-evidence items are retained and flagged, never silently deleted.
  • Evidence-context fingerprint. Every review stores the reviewer identity and role, the before/after status, the reason, feedback, the evidence-reviewed acknowledgement, an evidence-context fingerprint, and the model/dataset version. These are exposed in Decision History and in the JSON/CSV/PDF reports.
  • Nothing is automatic. The platform prepares and explains evidence; qualified staff remain responsible for interpretation, approval and action.
  • Traceability. Every decision records who acted and why, and is available in the Governance view and in exportable CSV/PDF reports and saved snapshots.
  • Method identity. The dossier states the actual recommendation method/model. Current audited decisions identify canonical_signal_weighted_baseline_v2; they must not be described as outputs of the blocked XGBoost candidate.
  • Committee PDF. A Curriculum Approver can generate a pack only when validated regeneration, eligible recommendations, documented reviews and explanations are all ready. The generated report is immutable and carries its report ID, source-manifest ID/hash and payload SHA-256 before the browser downloads the PDF.
  • Legacy review metadata. Historical review rows are append-only and are not rewritten. If an older row lacks a dataset/source field, the final pack states that limitation and anchors it transparently to the immutable validated-evidence manifest rather than inventing provenance.

Architecture and governance

5. Architecture

Three tiers behind one origin: the static frontend, the NGINX TLS front door, and the FastAPI backend with PostgreSQL 16 + pgvector. All decisions and validations are enforced server-side by the API; the browser renders views only.

Key components.

  • API routers: auth, admin, curriculum, labour_market, skills, analytics and ingestion.
  • Services: ingestion (including the paginated Adzuna importer with hard bounds), canonical skill mapping, XGBoost alignment on locked labels, baseline (readable-baseline-v1) and LSTM forecast evaluation, recommendation building, and the encrypted backup/restore service with manifest + verification.
  • Semantic matching: pgvector in PostgreSQL; a healthy state shows Vector Backend = pgvector, and unavailability is reported as not ready rather than silently falling back.

6. Data model and provenance

Evidence is stored as checksummed, versioned records traceable to a source file and ingestion job. Provenance runs from source record through canonical mappings, demand evidence, locked training datasets, candidates, active models, recommendations and decision history.

Notable artifacts: labour-ds-v1-<digest16> (fingerprint-locked reviewed-label dataset), readable-baseline-v1 (declared baseline model), and the immutable candidate registry entries with their metrics and gate status.

12. Security and governance
  • Transport: TLS terminated by NGINX (HTTP 8080, HTTPS 8443); the reference deployment sets REQUIRE_HTTPS=true and TRUST_PROXY_HEADERS, and DEBUG=false.
  • Authentication and authorisation: JWT with issuer and audience (JWT_ISSUER, JWT_AUDIENCE); RBAC enforced server-side; CORS_ORIGINS restricts browser origins.
  • Secrets: SECRET_KEY, SECRET_PROVIDER, SECRET_ROTATION_OWNER and SECRET_ROTATION_DAYS govern key material; provider credentials are Fernet-encrypted and never stored in .env or source.
  • Separation of duties: final academic approval is Curriculum-Approver-only; Administrators are excluded by design and cannot bypass it.
  • Audit: validation notes, mapping decisions, labelling actions, recommendation decisions and rationale are persisted as decision history and audit log.
  • Backups: encrypted bundles with manifest and verification; restore drills run against an isolated temporary database — the live database is never a restore target; retention is applied by an administrator.

Testing and troubleshooting

13. Troubleshooting and recovery
  • Start with System Operations. The page summarises /ready, /health and /api/v1/ready, vector backend state, and container status.
  • Vector backend shows not ready? pgvector is not available in the deployment; this is reported explicitly rather than hidden behind a fallback. Verify the postgres image includes the extension (reference: pgvector/pgvector:pg16).
  • Frontend serving unexpected content? NGINX serves only ./frontend read-only; re-deploy the changed static files to the host path and verify their checksums after transfer.
  • Service logs: use docker compose logs <service>; the deployment scripts also copy remote logs to /tmp (see the pull/deploy helper) and retain evidence under the backend evidence host path.
  • Stop/start/reset: stop-docker.sh stops the stack; start-docker.sh restarts it; reset-docker.sh tears down application containers while preserving named volumes. Confirm backups before any reset with data-loss potential.
  • Verify a deploy: compare SHA-256 of the transferred file against the local copy and confirm the served URL answers HTTP 200 with the expected anchors present.

15. Limitations and deferred work
  • synthetic Synthetic development snapshots execute the workflow but carry no empirical validity.
  • acquisition: technical UAT The Adzuna source was acquired as a trial (ADZUNA_TRIAL_NOT_EMPIRICAL, live_trial_uat); written academic research permission (2026-09-10) now applies and attribution to https://www.adzuna.co.za is required in thesis, publication, research or examiner packages. Historical pre-permission rows keep their original metadata.
  • deferred validation Institution-specific CHE/DHET evidence is pending until obtained; independent expert validation of model outputs is deferred.
  • regeneration blocked Validated evidence regeneration is currently blocked by the no_unreviewed_mappings gate (21 unreviewed skill mappings remain). Forecasts and recommendations were therefore not regenerated. The existing forecast explanations are reconstructed at read time from the current evidence base and clearly flagged provenance_basis=read_time_current_evidence_base; newly generated forecasts will carry an immutable generation-time dossier once regeneration is unblocked. Do not bypass the gate to force a green readiness state.
  • ESCO import accounting The Data Operations → Skills ESCO panel publishes exact, live per-file imported counts and relation counts for the measured ACA partial subset (checksum/version, linkage coverage). Files whose counts reconcile, files that are short only by within-file duplicate conceptUri rows, and hierarchy/reference files that are not separately countable are each stated plainly; the canonical catalogue remains 23.
  • researcher-operated UAT labels The locked reviewed-label dataset used for supervised training is produced by two researcher-operated UAT accounts under the enforced UQ58X relationship. It is not independent expert validation and must never be described as such; the replacement path is to operate the separate independent_expert_validation mode with genuine independent experts before any scientific claim or promotion.
  • source subset The ESCO v1.2.0 bundle supplied for import is an official-format partial subset (e.g. skills_en 13,960 rows, occupations_en 3,043 rows); it is not the full published distribution and the canonical FUTURE catalogue remains the curated 23 skills unless an explicit reviewed canonical-promotion operation occurs.
  • Short time series support prototype and development evaluation only. The LSTM requires longer historical sequences; forecasts are always shown beside transparent baseline comparisons and maturity status.
  • The platform never applies changes to a curriculum automatically; it prepares the evidence for qualified human decision-makers.
