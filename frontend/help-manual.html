Know your responsibility

What your role is for

Each role sees only the workspaces and actions needed for its responsibilities. Having access to evidence does not transfer final curriculum authority to the platform.

Viewer

Understand published evidence and decisions

A Viewer is a read-only decision user, such as a curriculum planner, manager, committee member, or invited stakeholder. The purpose of this role is to inspect trusted results without changing source data, models, or governance records.

A Viewer can

  • Inspect curriculum evidence, reviewed skills alignment, published demand outlooks, and decision history.
  • Open published recommendations and trace their evidence, confidence, limitations, and status.
  • Search, filter, and export the reports made available to decision users.

A Viewer cannot

Ingest or process data, create mappings, train or promote models, approve recommendations, administer users, or operate the system.

Analyst

Prepare and quality-check decision evidence

An Analyst combines the read access of a Viewer with controlled access to Data Operations. The purpose of this role is to turn approved source material into traceable, quality-checked evidence for human and governance review.

An Analyst can

  • Use every decision page available to a Viewer.
  • Ingest authorised curriculum and labour-market sources, monitor jobs, normalise records, and investigate quality failures.
  • Generate and review mapping or alignment tasks, apply the agreed labelling rubric, and document evidence-based findings.
  • Run approved processing and regeneration steps, then verify that outputs are complete before review.
  • Open saved evidence snapshots to inspect what a quality or readiness report captured at a particular time.

An Analyst cannot

Train, promote, reject, or roll back models; edit versioned normalisation code in the portal; change system configuration; manage users; or treat an analytical result as a final curriculum decision.

Data Scientist

Develop and validate governed model candidates

A Data Scientist combines decision evidence, Data Operations, and Model Lab access. The purpose of this role is to prepare reproducible datasets, train candidate models, test them against declared baselines, and document whether the technical evidence passes the declared gates.

A Data Scientist can

  • Inspect all decision evidence, reports, recommendations, limitations, and history.
  • Use Data Operations, perform independent evidence reviews, resolve mappings, and lock an eligible reviewed dataset.
  • Create dataset snapshots, start candidate-only training, monitor durable jobs, and inspect the model registry.
  • Record automated test evidence, evaluate promotion gates, document limitations and fairness evidence, and reject an unsuitable candidate.
  • Use Model Lab to resolve dataset, evaluation, and model-quality gaps. Operational preparation tasks are kept off the curriculum decision-support pages.

A Data Scientist cannot

Approve or activate a model, roll back the active model, operate backups or deployments, administer users, bypass evidence gates, or directly edit versioned normalisation code. Administrator approval separates model development from production activation.

Curriculum Approver

Hold final academic authority over recommendations

A Curriculum Approver is a senior academic or governance delegate with exclusive authority to give the final academic approval on curriculum recommendations. This responsibility cannot be exercised by any other role — including Administrators — and that separation is enforced by the server.

A Curriculum Approver can

  • Inspect every recommendation dossier: evidence, confidence, explanations, forecasts and decision history.
  • Record approve, reject or modify decisions with an evidence-based rationale.
  • Give the final academic approval that closes a recommendation's governance cycle.
  • Generate the immutable committee evidence pack and download its PDF after the readiness gates pass.

A Curriculum Approver cannot

Ingest or process data, train models, administer users, or change system operations. The role carries decision authority, not operational access.

Administrator

Govern access, activation, and operational continuity

An Administrator is the platform’s operational and access custodian. The role exists to keep the service secure, available, recoverable, and auditable while preserving separation between model development, activation, and curriculum authority.

An Administrator can

  • Use all decision, data, and model workspaces and inspect their audit evidence.
  • Approve or reject access requests, assign least-privileged roles, reset passwords, and review account status.
  • Independently approve and promote an eligible model candidate, verify serving continuity, and perform a documented rollback.
  • Run readiness, backup, restoration, deployment, service-health, and UAT checks from System Operations.
  • Investigate failures and preserve evidence showing who performed an operational action and when.

An Administrator cannot

Bypass dataset, validation, promotion, or audit gates; treat system access as curriculum approval authority; silently alter production evidence; or promote a model merely because training completed. High-impact actions require independent evidence and a recorded reason.

Everyone

Request an account and get approved

  1. Open the platform URL. The landing page (index.html) shows a sign-in form and a Request access link.
  2. Select Request access, complete the registration form (name, email, motivation) and submit it.
  3. You will see an on-screen confirmation that your request was submitted. Nothing else happens until an administrator approves it — this is deliberate.

Curriculum Approver (final authority)

Review, approve or reject a recommendation

  1. Open Recommendations. Filter by status or priority to find items awaiting decision.
  2. Select a recommendation to open its dossier: curriculum evidence, labour-market evidence, current-jobs evidence, confidence, SHAP explanation and prior decisions.
  3. Check three things before deciding: Is the evidence recent and from credible sources? Does the explanation make sense? Are limitations acceptable?
  4. Choose Approve, Reject, or Modify (edit content, then resubmit). A written rationale is required — it becomes part of the audit trail.
  5. Zero-evidence items are shown as non-actionable with a reason: you may reject them but the Approve control is blocked (and the server refuses approval) until evidence generation re-supports them. They are retained, never silently deleted.
  6. For final academic approval, confirm as the Curriculum Approver. Only accounts with this role see that control; administrators deliberately cannot perform it.
  7. Verify the decision appears in Decision History with your name, role, rationale, timestamp, the before/after status and the evidence-context fingerprint, and that the same chain appears in the JSON/CSV/PDF report exports.

Remember: approving accepts the recommendation into governance record — interpret evidence yourself rather than relying on scores alone.

You'll know it worked when: the recommendation's status updates immediately and the decision, author and rationale are visible in Decision History.

Analyst / Data Scientist / Curriculum Approver / Admin

Operate the complete model-to-decision workflow

  1. Prepare evidence: in Data Operations, complete curriculum validation and the Labour sequence: Import vacancies → Process skills → Review mappings → Generate demand. Confirm that only approved mappings contribute to demand evidence.
  2. Create reviewed labels: open Data Operations → General → Review, generate alignment tasks, record the displayed curriculum-to-demand label and justification, resolve any disagreement, and confirm the queue and class counts.
  3. Lock the dataset: create a reviewed-label snapshot. Record its row count, class distribution, manifest and SHA-256 fingerprint. A large labour-mapping count is not the training-row count: one locked row represents one reviewed curriculum-demand alignment example.
  4. Train and evaluate: open Model Lab, select the locked snapshot, start XGBoost training, and inspect chronological/grouped folds, the non-leaking baseline, class balance, calibration, explainability and stability gates.
  5. Respect the gates: retain a failed candidate and its metrics for technical evidence. Do not promote it merely because training completed. Promotion is optional and occurs only when every required gate passes.
  6. Generate decision support: regenerate alignment, forecast and recommendation outputs using the eligible method shown in the dossier. Confirm the displayed model/method identity; do not describe a recommendation as XGBoost-generated unless the dossier says so.
  7. Review and audit: in Recommendations, inspect the complete evidence dossier, record approve/reject/modify with a reason, and verify reviewer, role, timestamp, status transition and evidence fingerprint in Decision History.
  8. Export the final pack: when all four committee-readiness checks show ready, select Generate committee evidence pack. Keep the displayed report ID, payload SHA-256 and downloaded PDF with the examiner evidence.

The candidate proves the governed training/evaluation mechanism, but promotion remains blocked. Current audited recommendations identify their actual method as canonical_signal_weighted_baseline_v2.

Complete when: the locked dataset, candidate metrics, gate result, recommendation decisions, Decision History and immutable committee PDF can all be traced by identifiers and hashes.

System reference: Section 14 · Testing

Analyst · Data scientist · Admin

Run the labour-market workflow

  1. Open Data Operations.
  2. Select the Labour area.
  3. Choose Import vacancies.
  4. Use Preview request count to see how many Adzuna API requests the chosen settings would make before anything runs. Preview never contacts the API.
  5. Select the page size and start/end page range. Page size is 1–50; pages are 1–250 with at most 250 pages per run.
  6. Click Import pages. Runs longer than 10 pages require an explicit confirmation, because each page is one API request.
  7. Browse saved postings with Show saved Adzuna posts — records are listed 50 at a time with previous/next navigation.
  8. Return to Labour and choose Process skills to run the labour-market skill pipeline on the imported job-posting evidence.
  9. Choose Review mappings (or "Review mappings in Skills Alignment") and review candidate skill mappings against the actual advert text shown.
  10. Choose Generate demand to create labour demand signals from approved mappings only. The button stays disabled until approved mappings exist — approving candidate mappings is the prerequisite, and only approved mappings promote into signals/evidence.
  11. Open Demand Outlook (dashboard forecast view) to view the generated labour demand signals.
  12. Inserted vs duplicate counts: an inserted record is new and saved; a duplicate already existed for the same Adzuna advertisement and is reported as already present (not overwritten).
  13. Rerunning is safe: repeating the same page range inserts nothing new beyond the first run and simply reports the earlier records as duplicates. Nothing is overwritten.
  14. Date filters: if the upstream API has no server-side date filter, the portal applies the date range on the client side after retrieval, and filtered-out records are counted separately.
  15. Configured bounds are explicit: the portal never retrieves unlimited data. The server enforces page size ≤ 50, page number ≤ 250, at most 250 pages per run, and a request rate limit; the preview shows the exact request count and the wall-time estimate before you run.

Analyst · Data scientist · Admin

Complete the curriculum evidence workflow

  1. Prepare the source: use an authorised subject guide, syllabus, module descriptor, curriculum map, prospectus, or qualification standard. Confirm the institution, faculty, department, exact programme name, canonical subject code and name, evidence year, NQF level, credits, evidence type, and currency.
  2. Upload once: in Data Operations → Curriculum Documents, select the file and choose Upload Curriculum File. Wait for the green accepted-job message and keep the job identifier. The message confirms queue acceptance; it is not final validation. Do not click repeatedly.
  3. Wait for extraction: verify the ingestion job reaches Completed, then confirm the document appears under Curriculum Evidence with a sensible page/chunk count and active status.
  4. Validate the subject profile: open Subject Profile Validation → Validate / edit. Correct the reporting fields, especially the canonical subject code, programme name, NQF level and credits. Read the purpose and prerequisite text, add a concise validation note, select the appropriate status, and save.
  5. Validate the document evidence: under Curriculum Evidence Validation, open Review Evidence. Check the correct file/version, readable extraction, module coverage, outcomes and assessment evidence. Validate only when the evidence is sufficient; otherwise select Changes required and explain the correction.
  6. Resolve duplicates and aliases: compare the programme module total with the validated canonical subject codes. If two codes refer to the same subject, a stale/non-canonical module remains active, or the programme row has the wrong name, stop and request reconciliation. Do not validate both aliases merely to clear the queue.
  7. Generate mappings: after the profiles and document versions are validated, select Generate Evidence Mappings. High-confidence matches are auto-approved; uncertain candidates appear in the human review queue.
  8. Review every candidate: approve direct learning outcomes, assessed competencies, and clearly taught curriculum topics. Reject headings, document titles, URLs, bibliography entries, incidental word matches, and broken fragments. Add a short evidence-based note to every decision.
  9. Perform the final check: refresh the workbench and dashboards. Confirm zero outstanding subject profiles, zero unreviewed curriculum versions, zero mapping candidates, no duplicate active modules, and complete module metadata for programme ID, description, credits, NQF level, faculty, and programme name.

Curriculum preparation is complete when: the intended documents are active, subject profiles and document evidence are validated, the canonical module count is correct, required metadata is complete, and the human mapping queue is zero.

What comes next: this completes curriculum-side preparation, not the full alignment study. The national-to-institutional evidence chain still needs applicable CHE/DHET/SAQA and institutional evidence, and independent alignment labelling requires validated labour-market evidence plus two independent reviewers.

Analyst · Data scientist · Admin

Validate an uploaded curriculum subject profile

  1. After the curriculum upload finishes, open Subject Profile Validation in Data Operations. The default filter shows work that still needs review.
  2. Open Validate / edit and confirm the institution, faculty, department, programme or qualification name and code, qualification type, subject code and name, evidence year, NQF level, and credits.
  3. Read the extracted purpose and articulation or prerequisite text. Correct structured reporting fields when necessary; the original uploaded document and chunks remain unchanged as evidence.
  4. Check the row totals for extracted skills, learning outcomes, and assessment evidence. A zero means that the extractor found no evidence of that type; it does not prove that the source document contains none. Inspect the document evidence before accepting a zero.
  5. Select Validated only when all required reporting fields are complete and accurate. Use Needs review or Changes required when correction is still needed, and Rejected when the upload is not a valid curriculum subject.
  6. Enter a brief validation note and save. Validated and rejected records leave the default outstanding-work list but remain available through their filters for audit.

Complete when: the profile is validated, appears under the Validated filter, and its hierarchy, identity, NQF, credits, outcomes, assessment evidence, and skills are suitable for reporting.

Analyst · Data scientist · Admin

Label alignment evidence independently

  1. Open Data Operations and find Independent Alignment Labelling.
  2. First complete Subject Profile Validation so the curriculum hierarchy, subject identity, NQF level, credits, outcomes, assessments, and skills are ready for reporting.
  3. Then validate the source extraction. Use Curriculum Evidence Validation and click Review Evidence for a curriculum version.
  4. Read the extracted evidence chunks shown in the review window. Do not validate from the chunk count alone. Check whether the visible text is readable, from the correct curriculum version, and complete enough to support labelling.
  5. If the source/version is correct, extraction is readable, modules are sufficiently complete, and outcomes or equivalent evidence are present, mark it Validated, tick all four evidence checks, confirm the automatically calculated completeness score, and save brief expert notes.
  6. If the evidence cannot be inspected, contains spelling/extraction problems that change meaning, misses modules/outcomes, or appears to come from the wrong document/version, choose Changes required and describe the correction needed. Do not approve unclear evidence just to continue.
  7. After at least one curriculum version is validated, return to Independent Alignment Labelling and click Generate Review Tasks.
  8. Click Refresh Label Queue, then open a task with Open Review.
  9. Compare only the curriculum evidence and labour-market evidence shown. Do not use model predictions as the answer.
  10. Choose an alignment label: 1 = no alignment, 2 = weak, 3 = moderate, 4 = strong, 5 = very strong.
  11. Choose reviewer confidence from 1 to 5.
  12. List present skills found in the curriculum evidence and missing skills suggested by the labour-market evidence.
  13. Write a short evidence-based justification explaining why the selected label is appropriate.
  14. Submit the review. A second independent reviewer must label the same task; disagreement is routed for adjudication.
  15. After enough tasks are completed, a Data Scientist or Administrator locks the reviewed dataset for XGBoost training.

Complete when: your independent label and justification are saved, and the queue shows the correct stage. The locked reviewed-label dataset is complete only after paired/adjudicated reviews are finalised.

Prototype evaluation: synthetic or researcher-entered UAT labels may be used only to demonstrate that task generation, independent submissions, disagreement handling, adjudication and dataset locking function technically. Label them as test data and exclude them from empirical findings. If qualified independent experts were not recruited, report expert validation and the real reviewed-label dataset as deferred future work.

Why is my data ingestion job showing as "Failed" or stuck on "Pending"?

Pending means the job is queued behind other work — it will start automatically; no action is needed. Failed means the run stopped and recorded why.

To diagnose a failure: open the job row in Data Operations → Ingestion Jobs, expand the failure detail, and check the most common causes in order:

  • Duplicate file: the same content (identical checksum) was already ingested. The system blocks duplicates by design.
  • Contract mismatch: the file does not match the source contract's expected columns/format. Compare against the contract's validation schema and fix the file, not the contract, unless the source genuinely changed (contract owners can version contracts).
  • Unreadable file: password-protected PDFs, scanned images without text, or corrupt archives cannot be extracted.
  • Empty extract: zero usable records after parsing — verify the file is the correct document for the selected source.

After fixing the cause, use Retry. Failed jobs keep their diagnostics for audit, so nothing is lost by retrying.

What does the "Gap Score" mean, and how should I interpret it?

The Gap Score measures vocabulary distance between what a curriculum module teaches and what the labour market demands. It is calculated as 1 − cos(c, d), where c and d are TF-IDF vectors of the curriculum text and demand text respectively:

  • 0 — the texts are perfectly aligned in vocabulary (identical direction).
  • 1 — completely different vocabulary (orthogonal).

In practice: scores near 0–0.3 suggest strong topical overlap; middling values suggest partial coverage; high values flag potential gaps worth human review. It is a descriptive screening signal, not a verdict — wording differences alone can raise the score. Always open the gap evidence drill-down (curriculum, trends and current-jobs groups) before drawing conclusions, and treat SHAP-backed alignment scores and expert review as the deciding evidence.

I am a Curriculum Approver, but I don't see the "Approve" button on a recommendation. Why?

Check these in order:

  1. Your account may lack the approver role yet. Open your profile (top bar) or ask an administrator which roles are assigned; only curriculum_approver unlocks final academic approval.
  2. The recommendation may not be at the approval stage. Items still under review, already rejected, or awaiting modification do not show approval controls. Check its status and history in the dossier.
  3. You may be viewing a read-only surface. Decision History shows past decisions; actions are only available in the Recommendations workspace on eligible items.

If you believe your role is set correctly and the item is eligible, sign out and back in to refresh your session token, then reload. Access checks are enforced server-side — if the server does not grant the permission, the button stays hidden even for administrators, by design.

How do I reset my password or update my profile?

Password change (signed in): use Change Password in the top bar of any portal page. You'll need your current password; the new one requires a minimum of 8 characters.

Password reset (locked out): contact the platform administrator. An administrator can issue a reset from User Admin. For security there is no self-service email reset in this deployment.

Profile details (name/email): these follow your institutional identity and are updated by an administrator. Note that changing your email usually invalidates active sessions, so expect to sign in again.

Why can't I see Model Lab, Data Operations or System Operations in my menu?

The sidebar renders only the workspaces your role includes. Viewers see decision surfaces only; Data Operations appears for Analysts and above; Model Lab for Data Scientists and Admins; System Operations and User Admin for Admins only. This is intentional least-privilege access, not a fault. If your responsibilities require more access, ask an administrator to review your role assignment — they will assign the minimum role that covers the task.

A model is labelled "candidate". Why can't anyone activate it immediately?

Every training run produces a candidate: an evaluated but non-active model stored with its full evidence (metrics, cross-validation folds, baseline comparisons, fairness findings, limitations). Activation requires a separate, authorised promotion decision after gates pass. This separation prevents one good-looking holdout number from silently changing production behaviour, keeps an auditable trail of who activated what, and preserves a rollback target. Data Scientists prepare candidates; Administrators promote them — and neither role can bypass the evidence gates.

The XGBoost candidate trained successfully, but its scores are poor. Is the system broken?

No. Training completion proves that the locked-dataset, training, evaluation, explainability and registry mechanisms operate. Predictive quality is a separate question. In the current snapshot, only 40 of 993 rows are positive (4.03%); the worst chronological-fold F1 is 0, mean chronological-fold F1 is 0.0192 and ECE is 0.1184. Those results correctly block promotion.

Preserve the candidate and metrics as technical validation evidence. Improve future predictive evidence by adding genuinely different curriculum programmes or institutions, retaining real dates and groups, and obtaining more independently useful positive examples. Do not duplicate vacancies or relabel rows merely to improve a score.

How do I create the final recommendation PDF?
  1. Sign in as a Curriculum Approver and open Recommendations.
  2. Confirm the four committee-governance checks are all ready.
  3. Select Generate committee evidence pack.
  4. Keep the success message's report ID and store the downloaded PDF with the evidence package.
  5. Verify that the PDF shows the report and manifest hashes, included recommendations, decision reasons, reviewer identity/role, timestamps, evidence fingerprints and limitations.

If the button is missing, sign out and in again so the current role is loaded. If readiness is blocked, complete the named evidence or review gate; do not bypass it.

A validation screen shows zero skills / outcomes / assessments. Is my document missing content?

Not necessarily. A zero means the extractor found no evidence of that type — it does not prove the source document contains none. Extraction depends on scan quality and document structure. Before accepting or rejecting, click through to the extracted chunks in the validation window and inspect the actual text. If the text is unreadable or incomplete, choose Changes required with a note rather than validating blindly.

Where is the full end-to-end documentation for the system?

The complete ordered guide — all 21 workflows, the role matrix, the two overview diagrams, and the evidence index — lives in Decision Portal → System Documentation. Use it to trace any task from registration through source ingestion and validation to recommendations, decisions, and exports.

The guide records only implemented behaviour and separates four evidence classes: technical UAT (operator-run reproducibility tests), synthetic (simulated offline data), public evidence (official sources with provenance), and future institutional validation. Items that are not yet validated are listed explicitly rather than implied.

Can I rely on the forecast numbers for planning?

Treat forecasts as directional indicators, not guarantees. Every forecast carries a maturity status and explicit limitations based on how much history exists. Short histories produce prototype-grade output that proves the pipeline works but should not be quoted as predictive evidence. Always read the confidence, method and limitation notes shown alongside each forecast, and prefer the transparent baseline comparisons when judging trustworthiness.

Where do exported reports go, and who can see saved snapshots?

Exports download directly to your browser's download folder (CSV/PDF/JSON depending on the report row buttons). Saved Evidence Snapshots are generated reports stored inside the platform — find them under Reports & Exports → Saved Evidence Snapshots. Each snapshot captures what the system knew at that moment (creator, notes, payload), making them suitable for committee packs, audit and reproducibility. Snapshot visibility follows your role's report permissions.
